## What it is

The "hallmarks of aging" is the dominant scientific framework organizing the biology of aging into a defined set of cellular and systemic mechanisms. Originally **9 hallmarks** (López-Otín et al., *Cell*, 2013). Expanded to **12 hallmarks** in 2023 (López-Otín et al., *Cell*, January 2023). The framework is the closest thing to consensus the field has. `[validated clinical — as a framework]`

## Why it matters

- Provides the mechanistic map most intervention companies pitch from.
- Defines the surrogate endpoints (mitochondrial function, senescence burden, methylation state) that clinical trials in aging biology use.
- Bridges academic biology and commercial product claims — a supplement, drug, or therapy is increasingly positioned by which hallmarks it targets.

## The 12 hallmarks (2023 framework)

Grouped as primary (causes of damage), antagonistic (responses that turn harmful), and integrative (systems-level failure).

**Primary (damage accumulation)**
1. **Genomic instability** — DNA damage accumulates with age.
2. **Telomere attrition** — protective chromosome caps shorten with division.
3. **Epigenetic alterations** — methylation, histone marks, and chromatin organization drift. Basis for epigenetic clocks. See file 03.
4. **Loss of proteostasis** — misfolded proteins accumulate (e.g. amyloid).
5. **Disabled macroautophagy** — cellular cleanup fails.

**Antagonistic (responses that go wrong)**
6. **Deregulated nutrient sensing** — insulin/IGF-1, mTOR, AMPK, sirtuins pathway dysregulation. Mechanistic basis for metformin, rapamycin, caloric restriction.
7. **Mitochondrial dysfunction** — bioenergetic and signaling decline. NAD+ biology lives here.
8. **Cellular senescence** — "zombie" cells stop dividing but stay alive, secreting inflammatory factors (SASP). Target of senolytics. See file 03.

**Integrative (systemic failure)**
9. **Stem cell exhaustion** — regenerative reserves deplete.
10. **Altered intercellular communication** — chronic inflammation ("inflammaging"), hormonal shifts.
11. **Chronic inflammation** *(added 2023)* — separated from #10 for clinical clarity.
12. **Dysbiosis** *(added 2023)* — gut microbiome composition shifts with age and modulates the other hallmarks.

Source: López-Otín, Blasco, Partridge, Serrano, Kroemer — "Hallmarks of aging: An expanding universe," *Cell*, January 2023. `[validated clinical]`

## Mechanism subsystems most invested in by industry

### NAD+ biology
- NAD+ is a cofactor for sirtuins, PARPs, and CD38. Levels decline with age in tissues. `[validated clinical — decline]`
- **Precursors (NR, NMN)** raise blood NAD+ in humans in controlled trials. Whether tissue NAD+ rises in brain or other organs is **not established**. `[preliminary clinical]`
- Functional benefit in healthy adults: no large-trial confirmation as of May 2026 ([NPR, 2026-05-11](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/11/nx-s1-5813664/nad-infusions-supplements-longevity-science)). `[commercial / supplement]` when sold as a consumer product.

### Epigenetic drift and reprogramming
- DNA methylation patterns predict biological age (Horvath 2013; GrimAge; DunedinPACE).
- **Partial epigenetic reprogramming** — transient expression of Yamanaka factors (OSK, omitting c-Myc) rejuvenates cell identity without dedifferentiation. Mouse data robust; human trials just starting (Life Biosciences ER-100, Phase 1 cleared 2026). `[preliminary clinical]`
- Commercial reprogramming developers: Altos Labs, Life Biosciences, NewLimit, Retro Biosciences. None have human efficacy data on aging endpoints yet.

### Cellular senescence and senolytics
- Senescent cells accumulate with age; SASP drives inflammation and tissue dysfunction.
- **Senolytics** clear senescent cells. Two classes:
  - Small molecules: dasatinib + quercetin (D+Q), navitoclax (ABT-263), foselutoclax (UBX1325).
  - Emerging: CAR-T targeting uPAR (Phase 1 starting); DNA-aptamer-targeted senolytics (preclinical, Mayo Clinic May 2026).
- Mayo Clinic 2019 IPF pilot was first human proof-of-concept. Most subsequent studies are Phase 2, disease-specific. `[preliminary clinical]`

### mTOR / nutrient sensing
- Rapamycin inhibits mTORC1. Extends lifespan in mice across multiple labs.
- **PEARL** (Phase 2-ish, 48 weeks, n=114 healthy adults): primary visceral-fat endpoint failed. Secondary lean-mass and pain signals in n=8 female 10mg subgroup. Safety at intermittent low dose acceptable. **Not** evidence of healthspan extension ([review of PEARL, May 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=THhlMHJJWs8)). `[preliminary clinical]`
- Selective mTORC1 inhibitors (Aeovian Pharmaceuticals) attempt to retain benefit, reduce immunosuppression.

### Metabolic / nutrient pathways
- Metformin: AMPK activation, complex-I inhibition, hits multiple hallmarks. Subject of TAME trial — see file 04.
- Caloric restriction / time-restricted eating: well-validated in animals; CALERIE-2 (humans, 2 yr) showed metabolic improvements but is not lifespan data. `[preliminary clinical]`
- GLP-1 agonists (semaglutide, tirzepatide) — being studied for non-diabetes endpoints (cardiovascular, dementia). Adjacent to longevity by mechanism, not labeled as such.

## What the framework does **not** do

- Does not prove that targeting a hallmark extends human lifespan or healthspan. Mechanistic plausibility ≠ clinical effect.
- Does not adjudicate between competing interventions hitting the same hallmark.
- Hallmarks interact — a clean single-mechanism intervention is rare.
