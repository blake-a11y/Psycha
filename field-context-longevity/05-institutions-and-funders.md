# 05 · Key institutions and funders

**Phase:** 1 · **Scope:** in-scope · **Format:** Markdown with file paths

## What it is

The institutional spine of the longevity field. Academic centers, government funders, and large private foundations that produce most of the high-tier evidence and shape the regulatory conversation. Commercial companies are covered in file 06.

## Why it matters

- Whatever Tier-1 evidence the field eventually produces will come from these institutions. The supplement and clinic economy depends on their outputs.
- Hevolution and NIA together represent the field's largest funding bases. Their priorities steer where the next decade of research goes.
- For investors and operators, knowing which academic centers are running the senolytic / reprogramming / metformin trials is the simplest map of where validated evidence will originate.

## Major academic centers and labs

### US

- **Albert Einstein College of Medicine — Institute for Aging Research**
  - Nir Barzilai, Director. PI of TAME trial. SuperAgers cohort.
  - Centerpiece of the "treat aging as a multi-morbidity endpoint" regulatory thesis.

- **Harvard Medical School — Sinclair Lab (Paul F. Glenn Center)**
  - David Sinclair, Director. NAD+ biology, sirtuin biology, partial epigenetic reprogramming.
  - Spinout pipeline: Life Biosciences (Phase 1 ER-100 cleared 2026); historically Sirtris (sold to GSK).

- **Mayo Clinic — Robert and Arlene Kogod Center on Aging**
  - James Kirkland (until recent retirement), Tamar Tchkonia. First-in-human senolytic studies; D+Q originated here.
  - Active senolytic Phase 2 portfolio across IPF, diabetic kidney disease, Alzheimer's.

- **Buck Institute for Research on Aging** (Novato, CA)
  - Independent, longevity-focused research institute. Eric Verdin, President & CEO. Broad geroscience portfolio including senescence (Judith Campisi-founded program), proteostasis, mitochondrial biology.

- **Salk Institute — Gene Expression Laboratory**
  - Juan Carlos Izpisúa Belmonte (now Altos Labs / UCAM). Original mouse-Yamanaka-factor partial reprogramming work.

- **Stanford Center on Longevity** (cross-disciplinary, includes social science) and **Stanford Aging Mouse Phenome Consortium**.

- **UCSF Bakar Aging Research Institute** — established 2022, broad geroscience program.

- **University of Washington — Healthy Aging and Longevity Research Institute (UW HALRI)** — Matt Kaeberlein–adjacent network; rapamycin biology and the Dog Aging Project.

- **University of Pittsburgh + UTHealth — Sanjay Asthana / Aubrey de Grey-adjacent collaborators** — varied senescence and frailty work.

- **Columbia / Butler Aging Center, Brown School of Public Health** — DunedinPACE pace-of-aging clock (developed with Duke / Otago).

### Europe

- **Imperial College London — Babraham Institute** (Cambridge) and **MRC London Institute of Medical Sciences** — broad aging biology.

- **Karolinska Institutet** — Maria Eriksson and others; epigenetic and stem-cell aging.

- **CNIO (Spanish National Cancer Research Centre)** — María Blasco, telomere biology; co-author López-Otín hallmarks framework.

- **University of Oviedo / IUOPA** — Carlos López-Otín, lead author of the 2013 and 2023 hallmarks-of-aging frameworks.

- **Max Planck Institute for Biology of Ageing (Cologne)** — proteostasis, mitochondrial biology.

- **University of Cambridge — MRC Mitochondrial Biology Unit**.

### Asia-Pacific

- **Tokyo Metropolitan Institute of Gerontology** — long-running aging cohort studies.

- **National University of Singapore — Healthy Longevity Programme** — broad cross-disciplinary effort.

- **Insilico Medicine** (Hong Kong / global) — AI-driven aging drug discovery, $110M Series E in 2025 ([New Market Pitch](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/longevity-funding-trends)).

## Government funders

### NIH / NIA (United States)
- **National Institute on Aging (NIA)** is the dominant US public funder of aging biology.
- FY 2026 NIA Professional Judgment Budget request: ~$3.98B total resources; $113M new-research increment ([Granted AI / NIA budget, May 24 2026](https://grantedai.com/blog/nia-fy2026-ad-adrd-ai-tech-collaboratory-ncrad-nacc-infrastructure-consolidation-strategy-2026)). `[validated]`
- Majority of NIA budget tilts toward Alzheimer's and ADRDs; geroscience-of-aging-biology is a smaller (though growing) share.
- Key mechanisms: R01 parent announcements (PAR-25-332), R21 (PAR-25-331), K-series career awards, P30 ADRC centers.

### Other US federal
- **DARPA / ARPA-H** — emerging interest in healthspan-related programs; specifics vary.
- **VA** — Million Veteran Program data feeds aging-cohort research.

### International public
- **UK Research and Innovation / MRC** — UK aging research.
- **European Research Council** — large basic-science grants reaching aging labs.
- **Saudi Arabia (state-aligned)** — Hevolution (see below) is funded under Public Investment Fund alignment.

## Large private foundations and philanthropic funders

### Hevolution Foundation
- Riyadh-headquartered nonprofit. **Stated annual budget up to $1B** ([AFAR / Hevolution Conferences Fund page](https://grantedai.com/grants/hevolution-foundation-scientific-conferences-fund-american-federation-for-aging-research-a-491645e4)). `[validated]`
- Co-funds TAME-related infrastructure via AFAR.
- Direct VC arm (Hevolution Investments) participated in Aeovian, Rubedo, and other Series A/B rounds ([New Market Pitch funding data](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/longevity-funding-trends)).
- Largest single new entrant to the field in the last 5 years. Materially changes funding gravity.

### American Federation for Aging Research (AFAR)
- US 501(c)(3). Coordinates TAME, SuperAgers, FAST Initiative, MSTAR (medical-student training), Beeson K76 awards. Partners with Hevolution, Glenn Foundation, McKnight Brain Research Foundation.

### Glenn Foundation for Medical Research (Paul F. Glenn)
- Long-running aging research funder. Glenn Centers at Harvard, Stanford, Salk, Princeton, MIT, others. Postdoc + assistant-professor awards in aging biology.

### Other notable private funders
- **Methuselah Foundation** — Aubrey de Grey–founded; early funder of SENS-style approaches.
- **Larry Ellison Medical Foundation** (historical) — significant aging funder until folded.
- **Buck Institute Foundation** — institutional support of Buck.
- **Astera Institute** — broad longevity-adjacent grants.
- **NewLife (Bezos-linked rumored)** / **Altos Labs (founded with Bezos and other reported backers)** — corporate research with foundation-scale ambition; covered in file 06.

## Industry-funded but research-defining
- **Calico** (Alphabet, 2013) — internal research arm; broad aging biology; not a typical foundation but a major employer of aging scientists.
- **Altos Labs** (2022, ~$3B reported initial funding) — partial reprogramming; recruited senior aging scientists (Izpisúa Belmonte, Manuel Serrano, Shinya Yamanaka, others as advisors/consultants per public reports). Covered in file 06.

## Conferences and convening bodies
- **ARDD** (Aging Research and Drug Discovery, Copenhagen) — translational annual meeting.
- **Cell Press Hallmarks of Aging Conference** — academic, hallmarks-framework-centered.
- **GSA (Gerontological Society of America) annual scientific meeting** — broad geroscience including clinical and public-health work.
- **Longevity Investors Conference** / **Longevity Summit Dublin** / **Longevity+DeSci Summit** — operator and investor convenings.

## What this map tells you

- Academic capacity for high-tier (`[validated clinical]` and `[preliminary clinical]`) longevity evidence is concentrated in ~15 institutions globally.
- The Hevolution + NIA combination is the field's center of gravity for funding.
- The TAME trial sits at the regulatory pivot: AFAR-coordinated, Hevolution-co-funded, Albert Einstein–led.
- For an investor: pipeline visibility comes from tracking IND filings out of Mayo (senolytics), Life Biosciences / NewLimit / Retro (reprogramming), and Loyal (canine FDA template).

## Open questions

- Does NIA's Alzheimer's tilt continue to dominate, or does geroscience-of-aging-biology funding grow as a share through 2030?
- Will Hevolution maintain $1B annual deployment, or compress as Saudi PIF priorities shift?
- Will any state legislature follow Texas's psychedelic precedent and create a "longevity research authority" or equivalent?
