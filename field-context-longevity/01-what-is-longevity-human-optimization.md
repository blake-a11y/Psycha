# 01 · What is longevity / human optimization

**Phase:** 1 · **Scope:** in-scope · **Format:** Markdown with file paths

## What it is

Longevity / human optimization is the applied science of slowing, halting, or reversing biological aging to extend **healthspan** — the years lived in good health — and, secondarily, **lifespan**. Two overlapping fields:

- **Geroscience** — academic biology of aging. Mechanistic, hallmark-based, mostly NIH/Hevolution-funded.
- **Human optimization** — commercial and consumer-facing application: diagnostics, supplements, off-label drugs, clinics, wearables, self-experimentation.

The model is **target a biological mechanism (not a single disease), measure with biological-age proxies, intervene early**. Different from disease medicine, which waits for symptoms.

## Why it matters

- Aging is the single largest risk factor for cardiovascular disease, cancer, neurodegeneration, and metabolic disease. Slow aging → potentially delay all four.
- Targeting aging changes the unit economics of medicine: one mechanism, many diseases. The "longevity dividend" framing.
- Investor interest is concentrated and growing — $233.9M across 5 disclosed pure-play longevity rounds in YTD 2026, with 81.4% to therapeutics developers ([New Market Pitch, May 2026](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/longevity-funding-trends)).

## Key facts

- **Aging is not an FDA-recognized indication.** All current "anti-aging" drug development must use a recognized disease (e.g. diabetes, fibrosis, AMD) as the regulatory endpoint. The TAME trial is the canonical attempt to change this — see file 04. `[validated regulatory]`
- The dominant scientific frame is the **hallmarks of aging** — 12 cellular and systemic mechanisms (2023 update). See file 02. `[validated clinical]` for the framework as scientific consensus; individual interventions sit at lower tiers.
- The commercial ecosystem spans validated diagnostics (DEXA, ApoB, HbA1c), preliminary biomarkers (epigenetic clocks), supplement claims with thin human evidence (most NAD+ precursors, resveratrol), and self-experimenter protocols (Blueprint). Evidence tiers are not interchangeable.
- An estimated **~800 longevity clinics** now operate in the US, with annual membership pricing from ~$1,500 to >$150,000 ([Star-Telegram, May 2026](https://www.star-telegram.com/entertainment/living/article315797385.html)). `[commercial]`

## Current state (May 2026)

- **Life Biosciences** (Sinclair-co-founded) received FDA clearance for a Phase 1 trial of **ER-100**, a partial epigenetic reprogramming therapy, in optic-nerve disease (NAION, glaucoma) — the first regulated human partial-reprogramming trial ([Popular Mechanics, May 30 2026](https://www.popularmechanics.com/science/health/a71422623/aging-bodies-can-remember-how-to-be-young/)). `[validated clinical — early phase]`
- **PEARL** (rapamycin in healthy adults, 48 weeks) published April 2025 in *Aging*. Primary endpoint (visceral fat) failed (p=0.942). Secondary signals on lean mass and pain in one small subgroup. Safety at intermittent low dose acceptable. Not proof of healthspan extension ([summary review, May 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=THhlMHJJWs8)). `[preliminary]`
- **NAD+ precursors (NMN, NR)** — increase blood NAD+ in controlled trials but no large-trial human evidence of healthspan or disease outcomes. NPR coverage notes researchers describe human evidence as "nascent" ([NPR, May 11 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/11/nx-s1-5813664/nad-infusions-supplements-longevity-science)). `[commercial / supplement]`
- **Senolytics** — 30+ active clinical trials; dasatinib + quercetin (D+Q) is the most-studied combination, with Mayo Clinic Phase 2 data in IPF, diabetic kidney disease, and Alzheimer's-related cohorts. UBX1325 (foselutoclax) Phase 2b BEHOLD data published *NEJM Evidence* April 2025 ([Senolytic Therapy Market Report, May 2026](https://marketintelo.com/report/senolytic-therapy-market)). `[preliminary clinical]`

## Evidence tier definitions (used throughout this vertical)

- **`[validated clinical]`** — Phase 3 data or FDA approval supporting the claim.
- **`[preliminary clinical]`** — Phase 1/2 data, small RCTs, or observational data in humans. Directionally suggestive, not conclusive.
- **`[commercial / supplement]`** — sold as a longevity product; human evidence is thin, single-arm, or surrogate-only. Marketing exceeds data.
- **`[self-experimenter / anecdotal]`** — n=1 protocols, public-figure routines, podcaster claims. Not evidence.

Every substantive claim in this vertical carries one of these tags. Marketing language is not science.

## Open questions

- Does any intervention extend human lifespan, as opposed to surrogate biomarkers? No direct human RCT yet has the duration or power to answer this. `[open]`
- Are epigenetic clocks (Horvath, GrimAge, DunedinPACE) **causal** indicators of aging or downstream markers? Lowering a clock is not proven to lower mortality. `[open]`
- Will FDA accept an "aging-related" indication structured around multi-morbidity (TAME-style) before 2030?
- How does the commercial clinic model survive when most of what it sells (peptides, hormone optimization, IV NAD+) sits in `[commercial]` or `[anecdotal]` tiers?
