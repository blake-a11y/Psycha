# 04 · Clinical and regulatory status

**Phase:** 1 · **Scope:** in-scope · **Format:** Markdown with file paths

## What it is

The legal and regulatory frame the longevity field operates inside. Three facts dominate everything below:

1. **Aging is not an FDA-recognized indication.** No drug can be developed or labeled "for aging."
2. **All anti-aging drug development uses disease indications** as the regulatory wrapper.
3. **Most consumer longevity products are sold under DSHEA supplement rules**, which do not require FDA pre-market efficacy approval.

This is the central commercialization difference from psychedelic medicine, where FDA has recognized indications (TRD, PTSD) and is granting Breakthrough Therapy designations.

## Why it matters

- An investor or operator must price in: there is no analog to "FDA approval for aging." A liquidity event requires a disease label.
- The supplement / clinic economy thrives because the regulated drug pathway is closed to "aging" claims.
- The TAME trial is the field's leading attempt to crack a multi-morbidity "delay aging" indication. It has been pending for ~10 years.

## Regulatory pathways currently available

### Drug pathway (the only Tier-1 path)
- Sponsor selects an FDA-recognized disease (IPF, diabetic kidney disease, AMD, Alzheimer's, MASH, frailty-related fracture, etc.).
- Phase 1 → 2 → 3 → approval, with disease endpoints.
- Aging benefit lives in a "label expansion" or off-label use **after** approval.
- Examples: senolytic UBX1325 → diabetic macular edema; rapamycin → transplant rejection; metformin → T2D. All used off-label for longevity.
- Tier of resulting evidence: `[validated clinical]` for labeled disease; `[preliminary]` or weaker for off-label longevity claim.

### Multi-morbidity / delay-of-disease pathway (proposed, not yet established)
- The TAME trial framework (see below).
- Composite endpoint = time-to-first new chronic disease (MI, stroke, cancer, dementia, death).
- FDA has signaled willingness to consider, has not committed to recognition of "aging" itself as indication.

### Veterinary pathway (functional template, not human-applicable)
- Loyal's FDA Center for Veterinary Medicine submission for canine longevity drug.
- $100M Series C Feb 2026 ([New Market Pitch](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/longevity-funding-trends)).
- Establishes that a US federal regulator **will** approve an "extend healthy lifespan" indication — in dogs. Provides a public document trail and an evidentiary template.
- Tier: regulatory precedent only. Does not extend to humans.

### Supplement pathway (DSHEA, 1994)
- No FDA pre-market efficacy review.
- Health claims restricted, but "structure/function" claims (e.g. "supports cellular energy") are allowed.
- FDA enforces against adulteration and disease-treatment claims after marketing.
- This is how NMN, NR, resveratrol, fisetin, spermidine, urolithin A, peptides (until restriction), and most longevity SKUs are sold.
- Tier of evidence: `[commercial / supplement]` by definition.

### Compounded peptides (restricted 2024–2026)
- FDA has restricted several peptides (BPC-157, CJC-1295, ipamorelin, others) from 503A compounding pharmacy use.
- Many longevity clinics still source via gray channels.
- Tier: `[commercial / anecdotal]`; regulatory exposure for clinics is real.

### Medical device / diagnostic pathway
- Most biological-age tests (epigenetic clocks) are sold as Laboratory-Developed Tests (LDTs) or wellness products.
- FDA's 2024 final rule on LDT oversight (phased in through 2028) will tighten this. Expect biological-age test marketing constraints to increase.
- Tier: `[preliminary clinical]` for the test's analytical validity; `[anecdotal]` for clinical-decision utility of moving the score.

## The TAME trial (Targeting Aging with Metformin)

- **PI:** Nir Barzilai, MD, Albert Einstein College of Medicine.
- **Sponsor / coordinating org:** AFAR (American Federation for Aging Research).
- **Design:** ~3,000+ older adults, randomized to metformin vs placebo, composite endpoint of time to first major age-related disease (CVD event, cancer, dementia, mortality).
- **Goal:** Establish a regulatory precedent that aging-related multi-morbidity is a treatable, measurable endpoint. Not necessarily to add a metformin label — to establish the trial structure.
- **Status as of May 2026:** Postponed initially by COVID-19; still planned, funding partially secured via AFAR + Hevolution-aligned funders. **Not yet enrolling at full scale.** ([Popular Mechanics, May 30 2026](https://www.popularmechanics.com/science/health/a71422623/aging-bodies-can-remember-how-to-be-young/); [Life Science Daily, May 2026](https://lifesciencedaily.news/hallmarks-of-ageing-as-a-framework-how-the-research-field-is-evolving/)). `[validated regulatory framing; preliminary trial status]`
- **What it would prove (if it reads out positive):** Metformin reduces composite multi-morbidity rate in older adults. Implication: FDA pathway exists for multi-morbidity endpoints.
- **What it would not prove:** Metformin extends maximum lifespan; metformin works in healthy young adults; off-label longevity use is justified for non-diabetic populations.

## Off-label use — legal and economic implications

- **Legal:** Once a drug is FDA-approved for any indication, physicians may prescribe off-label. This is how rapamycin, metformin, GLP-1s, low-dose naltrexone, etc. reach longevity clinic patients.
- **Insurance:** Off-label longevity use is almost universally **not** covered.
- **Compounding:** Some longevity drugs (rapamycin) are compounded at lower doses than commercial tablets. The compounded supply chain creates dose-variance and quality issues — note PEARL's compounded-rapamycin lower blood levels as a confound.
- **Liability:** Physician carries the malpractice risk. Clinics differentiate on documentation, monitoring, and patient selection.

## DEA / scheduling

- Not directly relevant for current longevity stack. None of the major interventions are scheduled controlled substances (unlike psychedelics).
- Exception: testosterone (Schedule III). TRT operations therefore inherit controlled-substance compliance burdens.

## Recent regulatory and policy items (May 2026)

- **FDA repurposing comment period** — FDA-2026-N-4492, open through June 11, 2026, on repurposing older drugs for new uses. Mechanism that could carry metformin / rapamycin / SGLT2 toward an aging-relevant label ([RAPS, May 12 2026](https://www.raps.org/resource/fda-looks-to-repurposing-older-drugs-for-new-uses.html)). `[validated regulatory]`
- **FDA LDT final rule** — phasing in through 2028, will affect epigenetic-age test marketing.
- **Life Biosciences ER-100 IND clearance** — May 2026. First regulated US human trial of partial reprogramming. Indication: NAION / glaucoma (not aging). `[validated regulatory]`

## What this means in practice

- Drug-development longevity = disease-indication wrappers (senescence in IPF, reprogramming in NAION, mTOR in transplant + age-related disease, etc.).
- Consumer longevity = supplements + off-label drugs + diagnostics, sold cash-pay through ~800 US clinics and DTC brands.
- Bridging the two requires either (a) a TAME-style multi-morbidity readout that FDA accepts as precedent, or (b) accumulating disease-indication approvals until the off-label longevity stack is unofficially institutionalized.
