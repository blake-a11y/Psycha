## Primary research focus
- mTOR pathway biology and pharmacologic mTOR inhibition (rapamycin) as a gerotherapeutic.
- Dog Aging Project: large longitudinal cohort study (~50,000 enrolled dogs) plus TRIAD randomized rapamycin trial.
- Geroscience translation — moving from yeast and mouse to companion animals to humans.

## Core positions (3–6 theses they advance publicly)
1. Rapamycin / rapalogs are the most validated lifespan-extending pharmacologic intervention in mice and the highest-priority candidate for human gerotherapeutic trials. — basis: extensive mouse data (Harrison 2009 *Nature* onwards), small human studies.
2. Companion dogs are a far better translational species than mice for aging interventions; the dog-to-human translation is the missing step the field has skipped. — basis: design argument; TRIAD is the empirical test.
3. Most consumer longevity supplements (NMN, NR, "anti-aging" stacks) lack the evidence to justify their marketing claims; the gap between sold and proven is the dominant integrity problem in the field. — basis: public statements, repeated podcast positions.
4. Biological age tests have improved but should not be sold as actionable diagnostics to consumers yet. — basis: methodological argument.
5. The most likely first regulated gerotherapeutic in humans will come from drug repurposing (rapamycin, metformin, SGLT2 inhibitors), not novel partial reprogramming. — basis: trial-feasibility argument; contested.
