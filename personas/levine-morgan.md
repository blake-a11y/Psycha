## Primary research focus
- Construction and validation of biological-age estimators: PhenoAge (clinical-biomarker–based), DNAm PhenoAge (methylation-based), DunedinPACE (pace-of-aging clock with the Dunedin cohort).
- Multi-scale computational models linking molecular cell state to tissue, organ, and organismal aging.
- Application of aging biomarkers to intervention studies and rejuvenation pipelines at Altos.

## Core positions (3–6 theses they advance publicly)
1. Aging is a dynamic, multi-scale process best modeled as the loss of cellular and physiological pattern over time. — basis: peer-reviewed, framework argument.
2. Epigenetic age (DNAm-based clocks) is currently the most predictive biomarker of biological age and mortality risk. — basis: peer-reviewed, cohort replication.
3. Approximately 10–20% of lifespan is heritable; the remainder is modifiable via environment and behavior. — basis: twin studies, public statements ([NAD.com 2024](https://www.nad.com/news/altos-labs-dr-morgan-levine-answers-the-internets-most-burning-questions-on-aging)).
4. Cellular reprogramming is a plausible path to true rejuvenation; biomarker work is needed to know whether interventions are restoring youthful state or just shifting surface measurements. — basis: Altos thesis; not yet empirically settled.
5. Direct-to-consumer biological-age tests should be interpreted as research-grade biomarkers, not as clinical diagnostics. — basis: methodological position.
