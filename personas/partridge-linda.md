## Primary research focus
- Genetics and pharmacology of lifespan extension, primarily in *Drosophila*, *C. elegans*, and mice.
- Insulin / IGF-1 signaling (IIS) and TOR pathway as conserved aging regulators.
- Pharmacologic interventions: rapamycin, trametinib, lithium, combinations.
- Dietary restriction biology and its molecular mediators.
- Sex differences in aging biology and intervention response.

## Core positions (3–6 theses they advance publicly)
1. Lifespan extension is mediated by a small number of conserved nutrient-sensing pathways (IIS / TOR / AMPK); pharmacologic targeting of these pathways is a tractable strategy. — basis: peer-reviewed, multi-species genetics.
2. Combination therapies acting on parallel branches of the IIS / RAS-MAPK / TOR network produce additive lifespan effects beyond either drug alone. — basis: Max Planck rapamycin + trametinib mouse work, *Nature Aging* and *Cell Metabolism* 2025 family of papers ([PubMed 40437307](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40437307/); [Max Planck 2025](https://www.age.mpg.de/423298/250526_pm_live_longer)).
3. Brief or intermittent rapamycin treatment in young/middle-age animals can produce long-lasting healthspan effects. — basis: Bitto et al. *eLife* 2016 follow-up work; Partridge group *Nature Aging* 2022 brief-treatment paper.
4. Sex-specific effects of geroprotectors are large and underappreciated; intervention trials must be powered to detect them. — basis: lab body of work.
5. Translation to humans requires disciplined trial design, not extrapolation from invertebrate or even mouse data. — basis: stated career-long position.

## Evidence base

- **Preliminary / observational:** Combination geroprotector data is mouse-only; no human RCTs of rapamycin + trametinib.
- **Commercial / anecdotal:** Minimal direct commercial role; primarily academic.
