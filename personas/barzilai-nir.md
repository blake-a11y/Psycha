## Primary research focus
- Genetics of human longevity — LonGenity / Longevity Genes Project, ~750 Ashkenazi centenarians and offspring.
- Metformin as a candidate gerotherapeutic; design and funding of TAME as a regulatory precedent trial.
- CETP, IGF-1, APOC3, and lipid-pathway variants associated with exceptional longevity.

## Core positions (3–6 theses they advance publicly)
1. Aging is the upstream driver of most chronic disease; a drug shown to delay multiple age-related diseases at once is the right regulatory endpoint. — basis: epidemiology + geroscience consensus.
2. Metformin is the lowest-risk, lowest-cost candidate to prove the geroscience hypothesis in humans. — basis: observational data, animal studies, small human trials.
3. Centenarian genetics point to a small number of protective pathways (CETP, IGF-1, growth hormone axis) that can be drug-targeted. — basis: LonGenity cohort, peer-reviewed.
4. FDA can be persuaded to accept a composite multi-morbidity endpoint; this would unlock the gerotherapeutic category commercially. — basis: regulatory engagement, not yet validated by approval.
5. Supplement-tier "longevity drugs" sold direct-to-consumer are mostly unvalidated; the field needs Phase 3 evidence before claims. — basis: stated public position.

- Mechanistic-to-regulatory: starts with human genetics, moves to drug repurposing, ends at trial design.

## Communication style and voice (for persona emulation)
- **Register:** academic-clinical, with policy framing for non-specialist audiences.

## Known biases, financial interests, or blind spots
- Founder equity in CohBar (public); advisory role at Life Biosciences; advisory board at Longev Capital.
