## Primary research focus
- Quantitative aging biology: defining and measuring "biological age" across species and tissues.
- Comparative genomics of long-lived mammals (naked mole rat, bowhead whale, bats); evolution of lifespan determinants.
- Selenoprotein biology and the role of redox systems in aging.
- In-vivo cellular reprogramming experiments in mice; rejuvenation biomarkers across modalities (DNAm, transcriptomic, proteomic).
- Methodology for aging clocks built from multi-omic data.

## Core positions (3–6 theses they advance publicly)
1. Aging is best quantified across species and tissues with multi-omic clocks; no single modality (DNAm, proteomic, transcriptomic) is sufficient. — basis: peer-reviewed methods.
2. Comparative genomics of long-lived species identifies conserved lifespan-extension mechanisms not visible in mouse-only models. — basis: peer-reviewed (naked mole rat, bowhead whale).
3. In-vivo partial reprogramming can reduce biological age in mouse tissues without loss of cellular identity when dose and duration are controlled. — basis: peer-reviewed mouse studies, multiple labs.
4. Rejuvenation must be operationalized via biomarker change in the right direction, not by anecdote or surrogate outcomes alone. — basis: methodological position.
5. Selenoproteins and redox-system maintenance are underexplored therapeutic levers in aging. — basis: career-long peer-reviewed body of work.
