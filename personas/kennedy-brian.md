## Primary research focus
- mTOR pathway biology and pharmacologic mTOR modulation (rapamycin) in mammalian aging.
- Human clinical trials of rapamycin: dosing strategy, timing, and combination with exercise.
- Translational evaluation of small-molecule and natural-product candidates: alpha-ketoglutarate (AKG), urolithin A, NAD precursors.
- Lifestyle interventions: VO2 max training, strength building, GLP-1 / SGLT2 drug repurposing as healthspan tools.
- Development of clinically actionable biological-age clocks.

## Core positions (3–6 theses they advance publicly)
1. mTOR is a central regulator of mammalian aging; intermittent rapamycin dosing is the most promising pharmacologic intervention currently testable in humans. — basis: extensive mouse data; ongoing human trials.
2. Most current aging biomarkers lack clinical utility; the field needs a more actionable clock built for intervention monitoring. — basis: stated 2025 podcast position ([Peter Attia Drive #357, July 2025](https://peterattiamd.com/briankennedy/)).
3. Aging interventions plausibly act by "restoring dynamic range" — returning physiological responses toward the younger phenotype rather than slowing accumulated damage. — basis: framework argument; not empirically settled.
4. Candidate compounds — AKG, urolithin A, NAD boosters — are worth testing in humans with disciplined trial design but should not be sold as proven anti-aging products. — basis: trial-design + integrity stance.
5. Singapore is a strong jurisdiction for longevity clinical translation given regulatory engagement, NUHS infrastructure, and government interest in healthspan. — basis: program-building experience.
