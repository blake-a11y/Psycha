## Identification / verification notes

- All 18 named experts positively identified against primary sources (institutional pages, peer-reviewed publications, official bios).
- **Sinclair (#1)** retains the standard Harvard disclaimer that his affiliations are HMS, not Harvard University broadly. He is also a commercial founder (Life Biosciences), which his persona file already notes.
- **Johnson (#2)** has no medical or scientific credentials. Persona is tagged self-experimenter / public figure throughout.
- **Attia (#6)** holds an MD and trained in surgery at Johns Hopkins, but his current operating role (Early Medical) is cash-pay concierge longevity practice + media. Credibility class is "peer-reviewed clinical (MD)" by credential, **commercial / public figure** by primary current activity. Both labels are kept in the persona file.
- **Horvath (#4)** is now full-time at Altos Labs; UCLA appointment is emeritus per public reporting. Persona will note both.
- **Levine (#10)** is now at Altos Labs (Cambridge), not Yale. Commercial-scientist tag added.
- **Huberman (#17)** is a credentialed Stanford neurobiologist but the longevity stack he popularizes is not within his peer-reviewed-research domain. His credibility class is split intentionally — academic for vision biology, self-experimenter / public figure for longevity.
- **Patrick (#18)** is a PhD scientist communicating popular protocols; tagged self-experimenter / public figure for the same reason.
- **Kennedy (#14)** is the only person on the list to have held two CEO/Director roles at major aging institutes (Buck, then NUS Centre for Healthy Longevity).
- **Yamanaka (#11)** is included not because he positions himself as a longevity researcher (he generally does not), but because every reprogramming-class therapeutic in development rests on his discovery. Advisory utility = high.
